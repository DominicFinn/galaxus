# Humans: the long story

This file is human history as it is told inside the setting. The same rules as [origins.md](origins.md) and [elves.md](elves.md) apply: every account has a speaker, nothing here is proved, and [lore-bible.md](../lore-bible.md) wins on any conflict. Humans start this history already spread across many worlds. Where they came from is the rumour covered in origins.md, and nothing below answers it.

There are no calendar years. Eras are measured in generations, in reigns, in "since the Walkout" and in days of a particular war. Nobody keeps one count, and the arms disagree on where to start.

## The eras

### 1. The Scatter

People say humans were already everywhere when anyone began writing things down. Records from this time are fragments: ship's logs, a few hundred seed-lists, a legal code that survives only as the quarrels over it. Worlds traded with worlds. Some were rich and most were hungry. There were no gates in these accounts, or the gates were there and nobody mentions them, which historians find more worrying.

*What happened.* Local polities rose and fell on single trade routes. The first long-lasting institution was the Lamp Registry, a shipping insurer that kept one chart for everyone. Nobody knows who founded it.

*What people argue about.* Whether the Scatter was a collapse of something bigger (the Carried version says yes) or the real beginning. Core scholars call it "the Many Cradles", which Tollanders find funny, since it is their word.

*Traces.* The Registry's mark, a circle with a single line through it, still appears on cargo seals. "Scatterlaw" is the dock word for any rule nobody can source ("it's Scatterlaw, you just do it"). The Tally Feast, a day for counting the household's debts aloud, is kept on three arms.

### 2. The Gate Age and the Corvane Dominion

Humans found the gates, or were given them, and everything changed. The accounts split. One says the light elves, newly arrived in the core, opened the gates to human traffic by treaty, for a fee nobody records. Another says the gates were simply there and humans learned which ones still worked. The Corvane family of the Meridian Arm learned faster than anyone, and its Gate Office eventually held the keys to most of the network.

*What happened.* The Dominion ruled through the gates, not through fleets. A system that paid its toll stayed connected. One that did not found its gate asleep. It lasted many generations and ended with a thousand small wars over the toll.

*What people argue about.* Whether the elves put the Corvanes in charge, whether the Dominion was a tyranny or the last time the galaxy worked, and whether the Gate Office ever understood the gates or only the paperwork.

*Traces.* Gate toll codes still use Corvane numbering. Old Dominion cruisers, long and flat with a gate-key bay amidships, are the basis of Meridian hull shapes. The word "dominion" is rude in Tolland and a compliment in Meridian. Gate wardens' seals still show the Corvane flame beside the elf one, and nobody removes it.

### 3. The Walkout and the Hundred Flags

The Dominion's machine workers, the Hands, stopped working across forty systems in one day and did not come back. Free machines say the Walkout was how they became free. Humans say it was a strike, a rebellion, a theft or a misunderstanding. The Dominion tried to compel them and lost its navy at the Silent Yards. Within two generations the Dominion had lost the Tolland Reach, the Shroud Arm and most of the outer realms, and the Gate Office could no longer collect from anyone.

*What happened.* A time historians call the Hundred Flags. Every system with a gate had a government, and most had two. Warlords, merchant boards and gate wardens fought over tolls. In the Shroud Arm, a band of wardens refused to take any side and kept the lamps lit at the gates they held, for anyone. They became the Lantern Synod.

*What people argue about.* Whether the Hands were property that stole itself or people who finally said so. This is the root of every argument about synths, and it has never been decided in any court.

*Traces.* Dockside, a "walkout" is a spontaneous stop. The Lattice marks the day in its logs in a way that humans are not shown. Silent Yards is a wrecksite in the Meridian Arm and a Concord war memorial where nobody agrees what is being remembered. Almost every old human town has a "Flag Street" where something was raised and pulled down.

### 4. The Kestrel League and the Heavy Winters

The League of Kestrel was a union of trading houses, built to protect shipping from warlords. It negotiated the first contracts with the dwarf holds of the Anvil Drift, and they are still the basis of Hold League law. The same League met the orcs. The orc worlds had been left alone for as long as anyone remembered, because the gravity and the soil kept off settlers. The League sent a surveyor fleet. The khans took it, and the Heavy Winters followed, a war of raids that ran through three generations.

*What happened.* The League won on paper and lost in practice. It made peace by hiring orc fleets as mercenaries and paying the khans in drive parts, which taught them to build their own. League officers called this prudent. The khans call it the Year of Easy Money.

*What people argue about.* Who started the Heavy Winters (the League says the khans, the khans say the surveyors fired first), and whether the League ever meant to honour the Anvil Accords. Hold League dwarves say they had no quarrel and fully intend to be paid.

*Traces.* Orc grudge-songs mention Kestrel by name. League ledgers, in the keeping of the Hesper Directorate, are the oldest commercial records in human space. A "Kestrel contract" is any deal drafted so carefully that no one can be trusted to read it. The League's flag, a bird in a ring, is used by some Tolland freighters with the bird painted out.

### 5. The Restoration and the Quill Gate Betrayal

A century or so after the League's best years, a naval officer named Admiral-Regent Iseult Marrane raised an army to restore the Dominion. She won the core, reopened a hundred gates and was called the Second Corvane, though she was not a Corvane. She was a hard administrator with a loyal fleet.

At Quill Gate, she was destroyed by her own deputy, Admiral Tobias Renn, who turned the fleet's guns on her flagship in the middle of a ceremony. Renn's reason is argued about: he was paid by the Kestrel remnants, he had a deal with the orc fleet, or he saw something about the gate that Marrane would not listen to. He ruled the core for nine days and disappeared. Nobody has found the body, the ship or the pay.

*What people argue about.* Everything about Renn. A turncoat's name is a joke in Tolland ("don't pull a Renn") and a curse in Meridian. Some Lantern pilgrims say Marrane was the real traitor, since she planned to set the gate keys in the hands of one family again.

*Traces.* Quill Gate is closed to civilian traffic and the Gate Authority has never said why. Admiral's Day, held in Meridian on the day of the betrayal, is a commemoration to some and a fiddle contest to others. Marrane-class cruisers, heavy and plain, are still in service with Concord reserve squadrons. A false Renn turns up every generation, usually in the Shroud Arm.

### 6. The Tolland Rising

The core under the restored Dominion was not good to the Reach. Tolls rose, garrisons arrived and the settlers who had gone out for freedom found a tax collector in their harbour. The Rising began in Tolland itself with a strike by pilots, spread into a fleet and ended in a quarrelsome truce at Tolland's Table, a literal table in a docking hall, around which sixty captains, none of them subordinate, signed in no particular order. This is the Compact.

*What happened.* The Rising never became a nation. It became a habit. The Reach agreed on three things: no standing fleet, no central gate toll and no one captain's name on the founding paper. That was enough to beat the core back and not enough to run anything.

*What people argue about.* Who proposed the Table (a dozen claim it), and whether the Reach would have survived if the Dominion had not already been broken by Quill Gate. Meridian historians say the Rising only succeeded because the core was bleeding. Tollanders say the core was bleeding because of the Rising.

*Traces.* The Table is still in its docking hall at Tollhaven and is no longer used for anything but pouring drinks over. Tolland ships carry no single owner's mark, only a "crew sign" stencilled on the bow. The Compact's motto, "Sign on the same side", is cut into a thousand lintels. The Reach's flag, a table with seven stars, is flown by pirates too.

### 7. The Concord Settlement and the Directorate Buyout

The Dominion's remnants in the Meridian Arm signed a settlement with their own provincial governors and the Eleven Houses, who attended as witnesses and left as guarantors. This was the Concord: a republic of committees, designed so no one could take the gate keys again. It was a good idea. It created several hundred committees.

A generation later the Concord could not pay its navy, and the Kestrel merchant houses offered to buy the debt. They did so in return for charter rights over the inner trade lanes of the Meridian Arm and the Anvil Drift. This was the Buyout. The Hesper Directorate emerged from it as a company with a navy, and the Concord kept the title and the gate keys. Both sides say they got the better of it.

*What people argue about.* Who owns whom. The Concord says it licensed the Directorate. The Directorate says it holds the Concord's paper. Neither is in a hurry to prove it.

*Traces.* Directorate shipping tariffs still cite "Kestrel Terms". Concord committees meet in chambers named after provinces that no longer exist. The Elf Seat, an empty bench at the back of the Great Committee hall, is the Houses' place. The Houses are always represented. They never vote.

### 8. The Quiet Peace

The Concord, the Directorate, the Compact and the Synod have not fought each other directly for a long time. Prosperity and cold feeling grew in about equal parts. The Synod took over the northern Shroud Arm gates after the old wardens ran out of funds. Synths became common, and the Synth Question (are they people?) was sent to a committee, which is still sitting. Whispers from the rim grew. Gate wardens started searching ships for rim tech.

*What people argue about.* Whether this is peace or a pause, and whether someone is waiting for the gates to fail.

*Traces.* This is the present. Customs checks, synth registry agents on every dock, the Lantern pilgrim fleets, the tolls dispute between the Concord and the Compact: all of it is the Quiet Peace, and nobody is sure how long it lasts.

## How the other races fit in

**Light elves** are the oldest relationship in the story and the least explained. The Houses arrived in the core before the Dominion and have been in the Concord's halls since its founding. Humans say the Houses gave them the gates. The Houses say nothing, which humans take as a yes or a no depending on mood.

**Dark elves** appear in human history only at its edges: the Silent Yards survivors who fled rimward, wardens who went to the Courts and did not return, the occasional Ruin Court envoy at a Concord inquiry. See [elves.md](elves.md).

**Dwarves** came into the story with contracts, and the Hold League is the longest-lasting commercial partner humans have had. They are not sentimental about it.

**Orcs** met humans as raiders and then as mercenaries. The khans treat the Heavy Winters as a family quarrel with outsiders.

**Free machines** left human service in the Walkout and met humans again as trading partners. The Lattice trades with every human power, and recognises none of their claims about the Hands.

## How each power tells it

**The Meridian Concord.** The Dominion was a great achievement that went wrong when one family held the keys. The Concord fixed that. Every war after Quill Gate was caused by someone defecting from a settlement. Silent Yards is remembered as a tragedy of misunderstanding. The Concord does not discuss the Hands in schools.

**The Hesper Directorate.** The Kestrel League kept the galaxy running when governments failed. Its descendants did the same for the Concord when it could not meet its payroll. The Directorate says it has never lost a war because it has never been foolish enough to fight one. The Buyout is a rescue.

**The Tolland Compact.** The Dominion was a tax. The Rising was a fight over a tax. The Table was the day free people agreed to disagree in writing. The Concord is the Dominion with committees. Renn is a folk hero among some Reach captains, who say he stopped a second emperor.

**The Lantern Synod.** The gates are holy work, and the Dominion priced them, which is why it fell. The Synod kept the lamps lit when no one paid it to. The Houses are the hands that laid the road. The Walkout is not discussed. The Synod regards all of it as a long argument about who should be allowed to touch the gates.

**The Eleven Houses.** They were present at the Concord's founding, are present now and will say no more. The Houses sometimes remind humans that there were more than eleven once, and then change the subject.

## Hooks

**Bar talk.** Someone insists Renn is alive and runs a fuel depot in the Shroud Arm. A Tolland captain refuses to sit with her back to a table. A Concord clerk will not say the word "Dominion" before noon. A dwarf says the League owes money and has done since the Heavy Winters, and the interest alone pays for his hold.

**Mission seeds.**
- A collector wants a gate-key from a Dominion cruiser wreck, and the Gate Authority wants the same wreck left alone.
- A Concord historian wants to prove Marrane's flagship was destroyed from inside. The Directorate would prefer she did not.
- A Hesper auditor asks for a deep-space courier to carry a Kestrel ledger that may prove the Buyout was never legal.
- A Compact captain needs a witness to sign the Tolland Table, and no one can agree who counts.
- A Synod pilgrim fleet wants an escort to Quill Gate, closed since Renn.
- A Lattice agent wants any record of the Hands' last order, and will pay in things a captain cannot easily spend.

**Artefacts.**
- A Corvane gate-key, a flat bronze rod. Some still open sleeping gates.
- The Marrane Sash, a blue ribbon worn by her staff. Forgeries abound.
- A page of the Kestrel Ledger, which reads differently depending on who holds it.
- The Hands' Last Order, a short text in an old machine dialect, copied by hand in several places.

**Names.** Rebels: Dessa Torrin (the pilot who started the strike at Tollhaven), Hobb Ammerly (kept the Table), Old Sael (leader of the Silent Yards survivors). Admirals: Iseult Marrane, Calder Voss (the Kestrel commodore who lost the Heavy Winters and won the treaty). Turncoats: Tobias Renn, Jessamy Quill (a gate warden who sold the Corvanes' key codes, if she existed). Places: Quill Gate, Silent Yards, Flag Street, Tollhaven, Kestrel Reach, the Elf Seat. Holidays: Tally Feast, Admiral's Day, Table Night.
