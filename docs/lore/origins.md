# Origins: what people believe

Nobody in the galaxy can say where their own people began. This file covers how each race talks about the question. The same rules as the [elf lore](elves.md) apply: every account has a speaker, nothing is proved, and the real answer stays unwritten.

## Humans

Humans are old, and they have spread across four arms and thousands of systems. Even so, "all humans came from one world" is a rumour, and it appears in no school curriculum. Records go back a long way and then thin into legend, and each arm keeps different records. No chart, archive or gene library has ever pointed at a single place. A captain who claims otherwise in a bar will be bought a drink and argued with.

Three versions are held, and none has a majority.

**The One World.** The oldest and most popular version. Humans began on a single planet, and the world is lost, forgotten or hidden. People who believe it often call it "Home" with a capital letter and are mocked for it. Some Meridian families keep an empty place at the table for it, a custom that looks suspiciously like the one at elf courts. The Lantern Synod's position is that the question does not matter, since the gates are what deserve honouring.

**Many Worlds.** The Tolland Reach version, and the one most often heard from people who dislike the core. Humans arose in many places, or were scattered so early that the branches grew apart. Tollanders say the Concord likes "One World" because it makes everyone a cousin of the capital, and that a people who have to be reminded they are one people are probably several.

**The Carried.** A fringe version, told quietly in the Shroud Arm. Humans did not begin anywhere in particular. They were brought, by someone, along the road that became the gate network. Believers are mostly cultists, a few elders and the occasional gate warden. Most listeners laugh. Some stop laughing when they remember who built the gates.

None of the three can be squared with what the elves say, because the elves say nothing. A human who asks a light elf about it hears a ritual phrase, and a human who asks a dark elf gets a flat look.

## Elves

Elves never name a home world. Light elves answer origin questions with a few ritual phrases and dark elves with fewer. See [elves.md](elves.md). Elves treat silence on the subject as manners, and the rest of the galaxy treats it as a sign they know something.

## Dwarves

Dwarves trace their origins to a hold and a trade. Asked where dwarves come from, a dwarf names a hold, a craft and a contract. Asked where the first hold was, they say it was cut where the rock was good. The gates puzzle them more than any origin story does. They say a people ought to be able to say where its metal came from.

## Orcs

Orcs have the most definite stories, and the stories contradict each other. Every khanate has a founding saga that begins on a heavy world with a name, a mountain and a beast. The names do not match between khanates, and survey data confirms none of them. Orcs care very little. A saga is for telling at a feast, and a stranger who asks whether it is true has missed the purpose of it.

## Synths

Most synths know where they were made, because they carry a maker's mark and a date. Beyond that they ask the question humans do, with less patience. The rim-made synths, whoever they are, are understood to have no answer to give. Synths watch human origin rumours with wry interest, since humans are the only people who mind that they cannot prove where they came from.

## Free machines

The Lattice says its origins are myth even to itself, and the myths contradict each other in a way it appears to enjoy. Free machine origin accounts are entries in a log, and the entries disagree. A machine that says "I am older than my records" has told you its history in full.

## Writing guidance

Treat origins as an unanswered question that every race holds in its own way. Humans argue about it, dwarves find it a poor use of time, orcs sing about it and machines log it. Elves are the people everyone suspects of knowing, and they will not say.

Never write a confirmed answer. Never name a home world for any race. A character may be certain and wrong, and another may be certain of the opposite. Where a rumour needs a source, make it a person: a cousin, an old pilot, a clerk who saw a record once.
