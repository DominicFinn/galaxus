# Orcs: the Horde that never was

This file is lore as it is told inside the setting. It sits under [lore-bible.md](../lore-bible.md), which wins if the two disagree. The orcs of the Grav Khanates are certain that they once ruled a vast empire, and the facts say otherwise. Writers should keep the orcs sincere, keep the others sceptical, and never let a character settle the matter with a speech. The orcs are also not fools, and the file says why.

## What orcs say

Every orc knows about the Old Horde. Nobody is taught it, any more than anyone is taught that engines are loud. It is in the songs, the toasts and the way an elder says "when we held all of it".

The telling goes like this. Before the elves had a gate to their name, orcs held the whole galaxy from a throne on the heaviest world there is. The Horde marched out from the Anvil Drift and took every star worth the fuel. The elves were its tenants and the dwarves were its smiths. When the elf empire fell, orcs say, that was the Horde politely handing back a lease. The Horde itself fell to treachery, usually dwarvish, sometimes elvish, sometimes a particular uncle. It will rise again, and the khanates are the seeds of it. The Grav Khanates see themselves as the Horde in a long pause.

The proofs are shown with pride.

- **The Map of the Horde.** A tapestry kept in every khan's hall, with the borders coloured in. The borders cover the whole galaxy and follow the shape of the gate network with great accuracy, which orcs take as proof the gates were theirs.
- **The Standing Stones.** Ruins on a dozen worlds that the khanates claim as Horde monuments. Most are old dwarf survey markers or mine spoil. Several are other peoples' ruins that the orcs have been polite enough to adopt.
- **The Tablets of Kron.** Slabs carved with the Horde's laws. The tablets are in good condition, and the carving is a good deal newer than the stone.
- **The Names.** Orc epithets such as "who rammed the Ninth Gate" are said to be offices of the Horde and not nicknames, and each khan holds a few.

## The Goddess

The Goddess is a very large orc. Orcs say so without any embarrassment and expect no argument. Her full name is long enough to be a prayer by itself, so everyone says "the Goddess", and fond people say "Mam".

She made the heavy worlds by standing on them. Gravity is her weight, and a high-g world is one she leaned on affectionately. A drop of two g is a hug. This is why orcs feel at home under load and puzzled by soft, light places. It also explains the khanates' name for themselves, since they live where the Goddess sat down.

**What she wants.** Mostly she wants a good meal, loud company and an engine that works. She approves of seconds, of honest boasting and of hospitality. She dislikes empty plates, whispering, small cups and quiet engines. She does not much mind raiding, but she minds cheating at the table. Orcs believe she keeps an enormous ledger, and that it is entirely about who shared food.

**How she is worshipped.** With noise and with food. A prayer is something shouted at a bulkhead while eating. The usual devotions are the First Bite (the first portion of any meal is set aside, then given to whoever is smallest), the Big Chair (see below) and keeping the engine loud, since a ship's drive is the nearest thing to her voice. Orc yards build engines larger than the cockpit partly for this reason. It is a devout design choice, and it happens to work.

**Oaths.** "By her ample shadow." "By her second helping." "By the Goddess's knees." "Mam's bones." A solemn oath is "by her lap", and an orc who breaks one is said to have "fallen off the lap". The mildest curse is "may your plate be small".

**Shrines on ships.** A khanate ship carries a Big Chair: a seat bolted somewhere central, far too large for any crew member and always empty. Nobody sits in it. Crew leave scraps in it, tie ribbons to it and lean their tools on it. The chair is the Goddess's, and a captain's authority is partly that the Goddess is aboard. Smaller ships make do with a ladle on a hook, or a painted handprint the size of a door. Warships also carry a Hearth, a cooking stove kept alight in battle, because she fights better fed.

**Feasts.** The great one is the Long Table, held once a year at each khan's seat, where the khan sets the seating by rank and by grudge, and the seating is as binding as a treaty. Smaller feasts mark a launch (the Warming), a death (the Last Helping, where the dead orc's portion is eaten with their stories) and a settled feud (the Truce Pot, one pot of stew that both sides must finish).

**Schisms and heresies.** The faith has no head, so nobody can end an argument, and the faithful enjoy that very much. The main quarrels are about her size.

- The **Tall** hold that she stands taller than any gate, and tell their children not to point.
- The **Broad** hold that her width matters more than her height. Their shrines are low and wide, and their best preachers are large.
- The **Orbiters** hold that she is the size of a moon, and that moons are in fact lesser relations of hers.
- The **Proportionists** hold that all of these can be true at once, and are thrown out of most halls on the same night.
- The **Thin Heresy** holds that the Goddess is merely a tall, ordinary orc who has been exaggerated by the faithful. It is the one idea that gets a speaker physically removed from a feast, though always politely, and with a plate in hand.
- The **Hullists** hold that the Goddess is a ship. They are tolerated because their engines are extremely good.

Violence over doctrine is rare. Orcs are happier fighting about the khan than about the Goddess, and say she is too big to be offended by a mere argument.

## What is actually so

The Grav Khanates are real, and the Horde is not. Orc settlement of the heavy worlds in the Anvil Drift is old and well documented, mostly by the dwarves, who bought their ore and kept the invoices. Clans there grew into khanates over a long period. They have never held more than a few dozen systems at once, and rarely for long.

There is a kernel. Once, a khan called Ozgrakka the Unbent brought most of the khanates together in one season. The Feast of Nineteen Days was meant to crown her over all of them. On the nineteenth day the alliance collapsed over the seating plan, and the khanates went home with a very good grievance. That is the nearest the orcs ever came to an empire. Their historians remember it as the Horde's glory, and the fall as the dwarves' fault.

The rest was built afterwards. The Map was drawn later, from a gate chart bought from a dwarf who did not ask what it was for. The Tablets of Kron were carved in a workshop by a khan with good intentions and a nephew with a chisel. The Standing Stones are what they are.

Even so, the orcs have earned some of what they claim. They are good at the things they say they are good at. Their engines are the fastest of any yard's, and their ships are overbuilt in a way that works, since heavy-world metalwork is tougher than it looks. Orcs can organise a feast for a thousand on an afternoon's notice and win a campaign on the same budget. Machines are beneath their notice, which suits the free machines, who are used to being overlooked.

## What others say

**Light elves** decline to be amused. Asked about the Horde, a court elder will say that the Long Day had many tenants, and that orcs were among the more enthusiastic ones, and let the sentence end there. Elves find the Goddess charming in the way a very old person finds a child's drawing charming.

**Dwarves** keep receipts. A Hold League factor will produce, unasked, the invoice for the Tablets of Kron (carving, six weeks, one apprentice) or the ledger entry for the Map of the Horde (printed gate chart, one copy, sold at a fair price). They are not unkind about it. A hold that has traded with orcs for a thousand years respects them, finds them unbearable at a table and sells them the bearings for their drives.

**Humans** laugh in the core and stop laughing at the frontier. The Concord treats the Horde as local colour. The Hesper Directorate prices it as a nuisance, and its auditors have written some notable letters of apology. The Tolland Compact quite likes orcs, since both dislike the Gate Authority.

**Dark elves** say nothing, which orcs take as agreement.

**Scholars** are rude. The usual line is that the Horde is a very large claim built on a very small seat. Orcs reply that scholars are small and so are their seats.

## Rumours

Writers can use any of these. None is true, and none is false.

- The Map of the Horde matches the gate network too closely for a dwarf to have drawn it from a chart. Some say the orcs' ancestors did walk the gates once, in the elves' service, and remember it wrongly.
- The Goddess is an elder machine, a gate-keeper the size of a moon, and the orcs forgot what she was. Light elves find the rumour offensive. Free machines find it very funny.
- A dark elf court holds the real tablets and is laughing at the orcs quietly.
- The senior aunts of the khanates all know the Horde never happened, and keep the myth going because it makes khans cooperate. Orcs who hear this say the aunts are wise and that the Horde is true anyway.
- Ozgrakka the Unbent is not dead, and is waiting for the seating plan to be fixed.
- Somewhere in the Anvil Drift there is a stone with the true name of the Goddess on it. It takes six days to read aloud.

## Culture

**Khans.** A khan holds office while people follow. A khan is made by the Loud Count, a roll of acclamation, and unmade by the Quiet Count, when a hall stops answering. This makes khans rise and fall quickly, so a khanate's borders and its ruler can change between visits. The ones who last are good at plunder, feasting and listening.

**The Aunts of the Table.** Orc diplomacy is loud and visible, with envoy ships arriving at full burn as a courtesy. The real work is done by senior aunts who plan seating, settle disputes and decide who has not been fed properly. Nobody mentions that the aunts run things, and every khan knows it.

**Honour.** Guest-right is sacred. An orc who has eaten at your table does not lie to you or rob you, though they may do either the next day. Orcs lie freely about the Horde, which they do not count as lying, since to them "we were" and "we will be" are the same statement.

**Raiding.** Raid, trade and feud are kept in roughly equal balance. Raids follow forms. A raid on a rival's convoy is announced afterwards by a polite message with a bill.

**Engineering.** Orc yards build by enthusiasm and test by ramming. A drive is finished when the crew cheers, and the cheering is measured in a rough and informal way. The results work more often than the process deserves.

## Hooks

**Bar talk.** Orcs bring up the Horde unprompted and ask the captain's view on the Goddess's size. The right answer is any answer delivered with confidence. A captain who says "I don't know" gets a long, affectionate explanation, and one who says "she's small" gets a plate pushed gently away.

**Mission seeds.**
- A khan wants a Standing Stone moved to his hall. It turns out to be a dwarf survey marker, and the Hold League wants it back, with a receipt.
- A scholar hires the captain to photograph the Tablets of Kron quietly, and a khan hires the captain to stop her.
- A Table Aunt needs a rival khan's seating plan stolen before the Long Table.
- A shrine ship has lost its Big Chair in a raid. The chair must be returned before the feast, with whatever was in it.
- Two khanates will make peace if someone brings the right stew, and the right stew is in a system under blockade.

**Shrine-ships.** Any orc ship can be boarded and found to have a Big Chair. Crews react badly to anyone who sits in it, kindly to anyone who leaves it a snack and with delight to anyone who asks how big she is.

**Festivals.** The Long Table is yearly. The Warming happens at any launch. The Feast of Nineteen Days is kept in secret by some khans, with an empty place at the head of the table that nobody mentions.

**Named khans.**
- **Ozgrakka the Unbent.** Nearly united the khanates. The Feast of Nineteen Days is her story.
- **Mogg Brake-Less, who rammed the Ninth Gate.** The gate has a dent. The Gate Authority lists it as "maintenance".
- **Urzha the Unlost, who walked back from Ore Station.** Her tale gets longer each year, and so does the walk.
- **Rakka Patient-for-Once.** Waited eleven days for a cargo and became very rich. Her people still talk about it, with something close to horror.
- **Drog Well-Fuelled.** Raided a rival's wedding, stayed for the speeches and ended with an alliance.

**Sayings.**
- "Eat first. Fight second. Lie never, unless it's about the Horde."
- "By her ample shadow."
- "The engine is the loudest prayer."
- "Never trust a thin god."
- "We were everywhere once. We'll be back when it's convenient."
- "A small cup is an insult to the one who made it."
