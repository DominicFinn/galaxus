# Elves: what is said, and what isn't

This file is lore as it is told inside the setting. Nothing here is the real history, and the real history stays unwritten. Every account has a speaker, and every speaker has a reason to tell it that way. Writers can quote any of it, but must not settle a contested point or name the elves' origin world. Where this file is silent, leave the gap. The rules in [lore-bible.md](../lore-bible.md) win if there is a conflict.

Terms used below: the **Long Day** is what light elves call the age of the empire. The **Dimming** is their word for the fall. Dark elves call the same event **the Stopping**. Neither word is used by humans, who say "the fall".

## What the light elves say

The Eleven Houses tell the story the same way at every court, and it is meant to be told the same way.

The empire was the Long Day. It was a garden tended by eleven houses, each holding a lamp. The gates were laid like lamps along a road, which is why a gate is still called a lamp in formal speech and why a gate warden's seal shows a flame. The houses laid the road because they were already going somewhere. Then came the Dimming, and the lamps went out one region at a time. The houses who could carry their light inward did so, and they came to the Meridian Arm and the core. They say they came to keep what could be kept.

The account has fixed points.

- **The Lighting.** At the age of its first formal duties, a young elf lights a small lamp and speaks the name of their house aloud. The lamp is kept for life. House ships traditionally show one warm lamp at the stern when in port, and a dark stern lamp means the house is in mourning.
- **The Roll of Houses.** At gatherings an elder recites the Roll. It has two pauses in it, short silences between names, and the company waits through each without speaking. The usual ending is "and the rest are not spoken". Children are taught the pauses before they are taught why there is any need for them.
- **The Empty Chair.** Formal tables at the great courts set one place that no one sits in. Asked whom it is for, a light elf will say "for the guest who is late".
- **The Turning.** Four times in the year the lamps in every house are changed, and the season names are Thaw, Bloom, Harvest and Frost. Light elves keep this calendar aboard ships and stations where there are no seasons at all. Navigators have noted that the four seasons fit the year of no known world.
- **Ship names.** House hulls are named for light, song, seasons and birds, and builders call it etiquette. An elf shipwright will tell you the old ships were named for what could be seen from the hull on a good morning. Nobody asks which morning.

The light elves say the dark elves are cousins who took the night watch. In the formal telling the courts at the rim were left to keep the old lamps burning until the others could return. They say this warmly. They have not returned, and they do not discuss why.

If asked where the elves came from, a light elf answers with a phrase, and the phrase is always one of a few: "We were already on the road." "Before the road there was light, and we do not speak of before." "That is the house's to keep." Pressing the question gets a courteous change of subject. Pressing it twice gets silence and a cooler welcome next time.

## What the dark elves say

The dark elves tell it shorter and less often. Their account has one idea in it: *we stayed*.

The Stopping was the day the listening ended. Someone had to hold the door, and the rim courts did. They stayed in the ruins because the ruins were theirs, and because leaving felt like a verdict. They do not call themselves a power. Each court is a hall, its grudges and the few hundred people who remember where the corridors lead. The web of the Ruin Courts is also a web of debts: who kept whose gate, who owes whom for a winter.

The Courts sit at the rim because that was where the empire kept its rim courts, its watch-posts and, they imply, its oldest work. Ask a dark elf why the oldest gates are rimward and they say "because the oldest work was the hardest to leave." It is the same answer a light elf gives, in fewer words.

Ritual phrases are short and flat. "We stayed." "The door is shut and we are the door." "We do not count back that far." They answer questions about origins with the third. They will talk readily about the ruins and the people in them, and they close up when the subject moves to before.

Dark elf ceremony is mostly funeral. When a court's last hall closes, the keeper's key is given to a neighbour and the hall's name is spoken once, so it can be said that someone said it. The Ruin Courts' ship names carry the same habit: hard sounds, cuts, grudges and the names of ruins that no longer have anyone in them.

## What other races say

**Humans.** Most humans know the elves as the people who built the gates, and the idea of builders is an easy one to live under. The Lantern Synod goes further and venerates the gates as holy work, and treats the elves as the hands that did it. Light elves find this embarrassing and say so in a manner too polite to quote. The Meridian Concord keeps elf liaisons in the Gate Authority and does not say what they do. The Hesper Directorate values the Houses' money and has never learned anything from the Houses' records. Humans call light elves proud, which is true, and dark elves sinister, which is a guess.

**Dwarves.** Dwarf assayers say the gate metal is not any alloy their holds can make. The saying in the shafts is "hollow where it ought to be heavy". Dwarves respect elf joinery and resent elf silence about it, and will say the elves taught nobody anything in all that time.

**Orcs.** Some khans' sagas say the grandmothers of their grandmothers hauled stone for the elf lords and were not thanked. The khans who tell it treat it as a debt owed the other way round, and want it settled over a drink. Other khans think the story is a drunk's story, and cut in with a joke.

**Free machines.** The Lattice has an entry on the elves and will not read it out. Elves feel it. A light elf at a Lattice trade station will be correct and brief. Light elves say it is like meeting the grandchild of a servant who has quietly kept the house's books. The Lattice trade with the Ruin Courts freely, and the Courts say the Lattice pay in silence.

## Contested points

These are disputed, and the game should leave them disputed.

- **Whether the dark elves descend from houses struck from the Roll.** The two pauses in the Roll are said by some to be the names of Courts that refused something. Light elf elders say the pauses are for houses that perished. Dark elves say they struck their own names.
- **Whether the firewall was the last act of the light elves, the dark elves or the empire as a body.** Each claims the other did it, and each says "a hand that loved us" when pressed.
- **Whether the oldest gates are rimward.** Gate wardens say wear and drift suggest so. If it is true, the network was grown inward. Light elves answer "the oldest work was the hardest to leave". Few notice that this answer does not say whether it is true.
- **Whether light elves came to the core as refugees or as heirs.** Humans disagree about it among themselves, and so do the Houses.
- **Whether the elder machines were built by elves.** Elves will say only "we did not plant them".

## Rumours

These are the whispers a player might hear, from anyone, and none of them can be checked.

- That an elf's home star is a young one, and that a star so young would not have lasted this long. This is what the light elves' songs say about it, and it is why every search of the charts comes back empty.
- That the elves came from the rim, and the light elves are the ones who left.
- That the elves came from the core, and the light elves went home.
- That the elves have no world. They had a road, and the road was the world.
- That the empire's seat was never in the rim. The Courts keep what was left.
- That the Eleven Houses was once more than eleven.
- That a light elf who dies in the core is carried to the rim, and a dark elf who dies at the rim is carried nowhere.
- That the lamps are real. A house lamp is said to burn brighter when its house is alive, and the Roll's two pauses are lamps that have not been lit in a very long time.
- That the Unbound hulls look like elf ships seen in a bad mirror, with spires and thorns that look grown rather than made. Elves reply that no house planted them.

## What is never said

These are the elves' silences, and they hold for every elf in the game, whatever the writer's convenience.

- **The origin world is never named, located or described in plain terms.** An elf who is asked deflects with a ritual phrase, changes the subject or ends the conversation. They do not lie about it. They decline.
- **What they did, or did not do, in the Dimming.** Light elves say "the houses did what they could." Dark elves say "we stayed."
- **Whether the dark elves made a deal.** Dark elves do not deny it and do not confirm it.
- **The names in the Roll's pauses.**
- **What the Empty Chair is for.**
- **Why the empire needed a firewall.**

The feelings behind the silences can be shown. A light elf at a Lattice station is stiff and brief. A light elf facing an Unbound hull says nothing and leaves the room. A dark elf near the Hollow Gate, which spacers call "the last good gate", will not speak of it. Show it in behaviour and never explain it.

## Hooks

**Bar lines.** The grammar lines in the conversation files cover most of these. Others: an old elf who orders two drinks and leaves one untouched; a human who swears she was told the elf word for "home" and cannot say it back; a dwarf who tells the gate-metal joke to anyone who will listen.

**Mission seeds.**
- A house hires the player to recover a lamp from a ruined court. It will not say whose.
- A Court asks the player to carry a keeper's key to a neighbour, unopened, through space where ships go quiet.
- A Lantern Synod pilgrim wants passage to a gate that is said to be the oldest. The Houses ask the player to refuse.
- A Concord clerk asks the player to take a copy of the Roll to a Court, and to bring back whatever the Court writes in the pauses.
- A Lattice agent will pay to know what a light elf at a station does when shown an Unbound hull.
- A salvage crew finds a clock with four seasons and an orbit that matches nothing in the survey.

**Artefacts.**
- The Glass Lamp. A house lamp that glows while the house lives. Some glow dimly and some do not glow, and nobody wants to be the one to count.
- The Roll. Copies exist in human archives and all of them have the pauses.
- The season-clock. It tracks four seasons across an orbit no survey recognises.
- The keeper's key. A flat black key that opens a door in the ruins, or does not.

**Place names.** The Empty Chair (a dockside bar and a nav marker), Lampfall Station, Threnody Reach, Last Lamp (the nickname a Court uses for a ruin on the firewall side of the Hollow Gate), the Quiet Docks, the Glass Reliquary, Seventh Spire. Light elf house names in the data (Tirael, Vanthe, Selune, Aurelane and the rest) are the Eleven and their cadets, and the Ruin Court names in the data are the halls.
