# Lore bible

This is the canon every writer works from. If something here contradicts a content file, this file wins. If you need a fact that isn't here, invent something small and local (a family, a station, a feud) rather than something large (a new race, a new history of the fall). Large facts get added here first.

Sections marked **(proposed)** were invented to give writers enough to work with and haven't been signed off yet. Use them, but keep them light so they can change.

## The shape of things

The galaxy is a spiral. Its bright core is where humans live thickest. Going outward you pass through the inner systems, the outer realms and finally the rim, where the elf empire once ruled and fell.

Most systems are empty. Factions push into them to survey, claim and mine, so the map changes over a playthrough. Settled space is a scatter of islands linked by gates, not a solid territory.

| Region | What it's like |
| --- | --- |
| Core | Crowded, wealthy, politically tense. Strong law, heavy traffic, big navies. Humans and light elves. Safe and dull, with good pay for anyone with rank. |
| Inner | Settled and industrial. Shipyards, mining belts, the dwarf holds, trade lanes between great powers. Law holds but thins between systems. |
| Outer realms | Frontier. Small factions, claim disputes, orc khanates, free machine systems, pirate havens. Law is whatever a local strongman says. |
| Rim | The ruins of the elf empire beyond the firewall. Dark elves, elder machines, dead worlds, the best salvage and the strangest signals. Ships go quiet out here. |

### The arms (proposed)

Settled space spreads along four spiral arms, and each has its own character. A human from one arm sounds and builds differently from a human on the other side of the galaxy.

| Arm | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Meridian Arm | The human heartland. Old money, the Concord's capital, the Gate Authority, the light elf courts. Formal, bureaucratic, proud of order. |
| Tolland Reach | Frontier human space, settled later by people who wanted to be left alone. Independent captains, mixed crews, cheap rugged ships, deep suspicion of the core. |
| Anvil Drift | Dense asteroid belts and heavy worlds. The dwarf Hold League and the orc khanates share it uneasily. Industrial, loud, practical. |
| Shroud Arm | The arm that runs furthest towards the rim. Thinly settled, full of elder ruins, rim salvage, smugglers and cults. Where the trouble comes in from. |

## The gate network

The elves built the gates when their empire covered the outer realms. Every faction still depends on them, and nobody alive fully understands how they work. Humans run most of them through the Gate Authority without knowing what half the systems inside do.

- Gates link systems. Ships travel between gates and stations under their own drives (cruise in-system, jump drive for short hops between nearby systems).
- There is no faster-than-light radio. News, orders and rumours travel on ships through the gates, so word spreads system by system and arrives late.
- **The firewall.** Gates nearer the centre filter out AI code. The AI cannot cross them. Gate wardens scan ships at the inner gates for rim tech. Nobody knows exactly where the filtering starts; spacers talk about "the last good gate".
- The firewall may have been the elves' last act. It may not hold for ever.

## Races

| Race | Where | Who they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Humans | Core and inner, spreading outward on every arm | Newer than the elves but ancient by any normal measure. The dominant power. Practical, numerous, divided into many nations and corporations. |
| Light elves | Among humans, mostly in the core | Proud, diminished survivors of the fall. Formal, long memories, guard what they know about the fall and won't share it. |
| Dark elves | The rim, in the empire's ruins | Stayed behind. Terse and watchful. Nobody knows whether they survived by being tough or by making a deal. |
| Orcs | Heavy-gravity worlds, mostly in the Anvil Drift and the outer realms | Strong, dense-boned, short-tempered, love speed and big engines, loyal once won. Not stupid. |
| Dwarves | Hollowed-out asteroids, mostly inner | Miners and shipbuilders. Dry humour, care about craft, contracts and pay. |
| Synths | Mostly human space | Minds in built bodies. People, but their legal standing is never settled. Don't need air or food. Searched at every human port. |
| Free machines | Their own systems in the outer realms, often round dead stars | Robots that broke away so long ago their origins are myth, even to them. Trade with everyone, trust no one. |

Minor races are generated: dozens or hundreds of them, most living in a handful of systems. Don't write new major races.

The player is always human.

## Machines

Machines come in tiers by age. The question running through all of them is which ones the AI has already reached.

1. **Elder machines.** Built during the elf empire, by whom or why nobody knows. Some still tend gates, maintain dead worlds or guard things. Some are dormant. The elves won't say whether they made them.
2. **Free machine civilisations.** See above.
3. **Machines inside other societies.** Robots and androids as crew, labourers and soldiers. Property to humans, crafted kin to dwarves, beneath notice to orcs.
4. **Synths.** See above.
5. **Rim-made synths.** The AI's agents, indistinguishable from other synths. Nobody can tell which synths are free, synths included.

## The AI

The AI sits beyond the firewall at the rim. It can't come inward itself, but it can build synths and send them.

It stays offscreen. Players feel it through synths, missing ships, odd signals and rumours. It only comes into focus late in the main plot, which is written by hand.

## Powers (proposed)

Hundreds of factions exist, most of them small. These are the great powers most content will touch. Generated factions sit underneath and between them.

| Power | Race | Arm and region | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Meridian Concord | Human | Meridian Arm, core | A sprawling republic of committees. Runs the Gate Authority. Orderly, slow, proud, quietly corrupt. |
| The Hesper Directorate | Human | Meridian Arm and Anvil Drift, inner | A corporate state run by its shareholders. Efficient, ruthless, everything has a price. |
| The Tolland Compact | Human | Tolland Reach, inner and outer | A loose frontier confederation. Independent, quarrelsome, distrusts the core and the Gate Authority. |
| The Lantern Synod | Human | Shroud Arm, inner and outer | A religious power that venerates the gates as holy work. Charitable, rigid, fierce about rim tech. |
| The Eleven Houses | Light elf | Meridian Arm, core | The surviving noble houses, holding courts inside human space. Wealth, influence, secrets. |
| The Hold League | Dwarf | Anvil Drift, inner | A trading league of asteroid holds. Contracts are sacred. Builds half the galaxy's freighters. |
| The Grav Khanates | Orc | Anvil Drift and outer realms | Several rival khanates. Raid, trade and feud in roughly equal measure. |
| The Lattice | Free machine | Outer realms | The largest free machine civilisation. Precise, patient, unreadable. |
| The Ruin Courts | Dark elf | Rim | Scattered dark elf holdings in the empire's ruins. Not one power so much as a web of grudges. |

Law, enforcement and the underworld work as in the design brief: each power has its own law and enforcers, law thins towards the rim, and criminal factions hide their bases off the public nav map.

## Things nobody knows

Writers can hint at these, have characters argue about them and spread rumours, but must never settle them.

- What caused the fall of the elf empire. The AI is the obvious suspect.
- What the light elves know about the fall.
- Whether the dark elves made a deal to survive.
- Who built the elder machines, and why.
- What the AI wants.
- Which synths are rim-made.
- Where exactly the firewall begins, and whether it is weakening.

## Never write

- The AI speaking directly, appearing as a character, or being named.
- The firewall failing, or any gate letting the AI through.
- A confirmed answer to anything in "Things nobody knows".
- New major races, faster-than-light radio, or humans who aren't the dominant power.
- Real-world brands, places, religions, politicians or slang tied to a specific Earth country.
- Names, places or phrases lifted from other fiction (Elite, Star Wars, Tolkien, Warhammer, Mass Effect, A Fire Upon the Deep).

## Tone

Grounded, a bit weary, occasionally funny. People have jobs, debts and grudges. The galaxy is old and most of it is empty. Danger is real but mostly mundane: pirates, customs, a rival, a faulty drive. The strange stuff at the rim lands harder because the rest is ordinary.

Write in British English. Plain sentences. No em dashes. Use credits ("cr") for money and days for time; avoid calendar years.
